Privacy

Where your family’s details actually go

Short version: they stay in your browser. But you are recording other people’s lives here, so the long version is worth two minutes.

What Heirloom keeps, and where

Everything you enter — names, dates, places, occupations, relationships and photos — is saved in your own browser under the key heirloom.v1. There is no account, no server, and no database belonging to me. Close the tab and the data is still on that device; open Heirloom on a different device and it will be empty, because nothing was ever uploaded.

Photos you add are embedded directly into that saved data, not linked from somewhere else. That matters for sharing — see below.

What never leaves your device

The app makes no network requests carrying your data. There is no analytics, no tracking pixel, no error reporting, and no “anonymous usage statistics”. Printing, PDF export, PNG export and spreadsheet import all happen locally.

The three things that do reach the internet

Stated plainly, because “fully private” would be a lie:

  • Typefaces. Every visit loads fonts from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com). Google therefore sees your IP address and browser — the same as on most of the web. None of your family data is included.
  • QR codes. If you generate one, a small QR library loads from cdnjs.cloudflare.com. The QR code itself is drawn on your device; the link inside it is never sent anywhere.
  • Voice input. If you use the microphone button, your browser’s own speech service does the transcription. In Chrome that means the audio is sent to Google. Don’t dictate anything you would not say in front of Google — type it instead.

Share links deserve real care

When you share a tree, the whole family is compressed into the part of the link after the #. Browsers never transmit that part to a web server, so the data genuinely travels inside the link rather than through me or anyone else.

The flip side: anyone holding that link holds the entire family — every name, date of birth, place and photo in it. The link does not expire, cannot be revoked, and will keep working after you forget you sent it. Treat it exactly as you would treat the data itself, and think before posting one in a large group.

You are recording living people

A family tree is full of other people’s personal information: dates of birth, locations, photographs, sometimes health or relationship details. Under India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, and simple good manners, that is theirs and not yours. Ask relatives before adding their date of birth or photograph, and before sharing a tree that contains them. Anyone can ask you to take their details out.

Deleting everything

Use Clear everything inside the app, or clear site data for this page in your browser settings. That is the whole deletion process — there is no copy elsewhere for me to remove, and no request for me to process, because I never had it.
